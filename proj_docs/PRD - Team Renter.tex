\section{Product Overview}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Description}

LivedIn is a \textbf{two-sided verified review platform} for rental properties that reduces risk for tenants pre-lease signing and gives landlords actionable insight into their portfolio reputation.

\textbf{For renters}, the platform scores rental properties on an objective scale using five structured metrics. By enforcing structure and verification, the platform minimises fake or retaliatory reviews and gives tenants reliable, comparable signals when evaluating rental properties before signing a lease.

\textbf{For landlords and property managers}, the platform provides a Business Portal with portfolio analytics, benchmark comparisons, review response capabilities, renter sentiment tracking, and team access management. Landlords can monitor and improve their reputation through data-driven insights rather than guesswork.

\subsection{Two-Sided Marketplace Model}

LivedIn operates as a two-sided trust marketplace:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Renters} browse properties, read trust scores and category breakdowns, compare options, and submit verified reviews.
    \item \textbf{Landlords/Property Managers} monitor portfolio performance, track renter sentiment, respond to reviews (subject to admin approval), identify review gaps, and manage team access.
\end{itemize}

Both sides benefit from the same underlying data: verified, structured reviews aggregated into trust scores. Renters produce the data; landlords consume and act on it.

\subsection{User Problem}

Existing tenant screening practices focus on financial and legal eligibility, but tenants currently have no reliable way to evaluate the \emph{lived experience} of a rental property before committing to a lease.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Renters---especially first-time renters---rely on subjective ``vibes'' during showings and word-of-mouth, with no structured comparison tool.
    \item Landlords have no visibility into how their properties are perceived relative to competitors, and no channel to address tenant concerns proactively.
    \item Market scarcity pressures tenants to commit quickly to properties they are unsure of.
\end{itemize}

LivedIn addresses both sides:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Renters} get verified, experience-based insights to make informed decisions.
    \item \textbf{Landlords} get a reputation management tool to monitor, benchmark, and improve.
\end{itemize}

\section{End Users}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Primary Persona: Renter (Review Searcher)}

Renters who browse the platform to screen properties based on verified reviews and trust scores before signing a lease. This includes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item First-time renters without prior experience to rely on
    \item Renters relocating to a new city or neighbourhood
    \item Renters comparing multiple properties simultaneously
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Secondary Persona: Renter (Review Leaver)}

Renters who submit structured reviews about their living experience at a property, contributing to the platform's data layer.

\subsection{Tertiary Persona: Landlord / Property Manager}

\textbf{(New in v2.0)} Landlords and property managers who use the Business Portal to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Monitor portfolio trust scores and review volume across their properties
    \item View category-level performance analytics and trend data
    \item Benchmark their properties against city and neighbourhood averages
    \item Track renter sentiment signals (common themes, sentiment trends)
    \item Respond to reviews (subject to admin moderation)
    \item Identify review gaps and generate tenant invite links
    \item Manage team access with role-based permissions (viewer, editor, admin)
    \item Maintain company profile and notification preferences
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Landlord Jobs-to-Be-Done}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Understand reputation}: ``How do my properties score relative to the market?''
    \item \textbf{Identify weaknesses}: ``Which categories are dragging my trust score down?''
    \item \textbf{Respond to feedback}: ``A renter flagged maintenance delays---I want to acknowledge and address it.''
    \item \textbf{Fill review gaps}: ``My newest property has zero reviews---I need to encourage tenants to contribute.''
    \item \textbf{Track trends}: ``Is my portfolio improving or declining over time?''
    \item \textbf{Delegate}: ``My property manager needs access to review data but not team settings.''
\end{enumerate}

\section{Customer Discovery Highlights}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Renters want trust signals before committing}: responsiveness, professionalism, maintenance reliability, safety/security. They value predictability---clear rules, costs, fewer surprises.
    \item \textbf{Living experience is unknown until after move-in}: decisions rely on subjective impressions during showings. Market scarcity leads tenants to commit faster to properties they are unsure of.
    \item \textbf{Walk-throughs take significant time for landlords}: they want serious, informed interest from tenants, not tyre-kickers.
    \item \textbf{Landlords want visibility into their reputation}: property managers expressed interest in understanding how their properties are perceived and how to improve.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════

This project delivers a rental trust platform that enables landlords and renters to exchange verified, non-financial trust signals alongside standard screening practices.

\subsection{In Scope}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Verified review submission with structured five-metric scoring
    \item Public property browsing, search, and trust score display
    \item Property detail pages with category breakdowns and distilled insights
    \item \textbf{Neighbourhood browsing and detail pages} (new)
    \item \textbf{Property comparison tool} (new)
    \item \textbf{Renter dashboard with shortlist and review history} (new)
    \item \textbf{Business Portal for landlords/property managers} (new)
    \item Admin moderation, audit, and property management
    \item Authentication (email/password + Google OAuth)
    \item Anti-abuse controls (rate limits, one review per property per user, moderation)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out of Scope}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Residency verification (identity documents, proof of address) --- still out of scope
    \item Payment processing for landlord subscriptions (business model, not technical scope)
    \item Real-time notification delivery system (email/push infrastructure)
    \item Map-based property search with geolocation pins
    \item Implementing the New FE prototypes as standalone Vite SPAs
    \item Adopting the seven-category rating model from the prototype mock data
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
    \item RentFaster is the established go-to platform for rental needs in the target market
    \item Building credibility from scratch remains a major constraint
    \item Focus on mitigation of abuse instead of inability
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Assumptions}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Renters will engage with external platforms if it saves time and reduces risk
    \item Landlords will want to push the platform to their tenants for reputation visibility
    \item The market is difficult and current information asymmetry is sufficient motivation
\end{itemize}

\section{Rating Model (Canonical)}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════

The platform uses a \textbf{five-metric structured scoring model}. Each metric is rated on a 0--5 scale in half-star increments (0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, \ldots, 5). An overall \textbf{TrustScore} is computed as a weighted aggregate of the five metrics.

\begin{longtable}{p{4.5cm} p{8.5cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Prompt / Definition} \\
\midrule
\endhead
Management Responsiveness &
``After you reported an issue, how quickly did the landlord or property manager typically respond?'' \\
\addlinespace
Maintenance Timeliness &
``If there were maintenance issues, were they resolved within a reasonable timeframe?'' \\
\addlinespace
Listing Accuracy &
``When you moved in, did the property match what was advertised?'' \\
\addlinespace
Fee Transparency &
``Were all fees, charges, and financial obligations communicated clearly and applied consistently?'' \\
\addlinespace
Lease Clarity &
``Were expectations, rules, and processes for tenancy communicated clearly and followed reliably?'' \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\subsubsection{Decision Record: Seven-Category Alternative Rejected}

The New FE prototypes proposed a seven-category model (\texttt{maintenance}, \texttt{responsiveness}, \texttt{value}, \texttt{safety}, \texttt{noise}, \texttt{moveInOut}, \texttt{cleanliness}). This model is \textbf{rejected} because:
\begin{itemize}
    \item It is incompatible with the existing database schema, validation logic, and aggregate pipeline
    \item It introduces subjective categories (``noise'', ``cleanliness'') that overlap with existing metrics or fall outside the platform's structured-trust focus
    \item Changing the metric model at this stage would require rewriting migrations, RLS policies, validation schemas, and all public aggregate display logic
\end{itemize}

The five-metric model remains canonical for all surfaces (public, portal, admin).

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l X}

\midrule

\bottomrule

\subsection{Framework Decision Record}

\textbf{Decision}: Next.js App Router is the single framework for all surfaces---public, Business Portal, and admin.

\textbf{Rationale}: The New FE prototypes (Facelift and Business Portal) were built as Vite + React Router SPAs with mock data. Adopting them as parallel stacks would create:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Framework fragmentation (Next.js vs.\ Vite)
    \item Duplicated auth, data fetching, and type systems
    \item Two deployment pipelines
    \item Loss of SSR, ISR, and server components
\end{itemize}

Instead, the UX patterns and feature ideas from the prototypes are adopted into the existing Next.js architecture. The prototypes serve as design references, not production code.

\subsection{Architecture}

\begin{verbatim}
+--------------------+     +----------------------------------+
| Browser / Client   |<--->| Next.js App (UI + API Routes)    |
| - Public browsing  |     | - Server Components (pages)      |

          v                             v
+--------------------+     +-------------------------------+
| Supabase Postgres  |<--->| Supabase Auth                 |

| - reviews          | RLS | - email verification          |
| - aggregates       |     | - roles: public/verified/     |
| - insights         |     |   admin/landlord              |
| - portfolios       |     +-------------------------------+
| - neighbourhoods   |

+---------+----------+
          |
          | (C) triggers on review/moderation
          v

\subsection{Data Layer}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Supabase Postgres is the single source of truth for all data
    \item All tables have RLS enabled from creation
    \item No direct client-side Supabase access from the Business Portal---all data flows through Next.js API routes
    \item Server components fetch data server-side; client components use auth-aware fetch wrappers
    \item The \texttt{landlord} role is portfolio-scoped: landlords can only access properties linked to them via \texttt{portfolio\_properties}
\end{itemize}

\section{User Flows}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{User A: Searching Reviews}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Landing / Home page
    \item Enter address, property name, or browse by neighbourhood
    \item Filter and sort results
    \item Select property to view detail
    \item Review trust score, category breakdown, and distilled insights
    \item Optionally compare with other properties
    \item Optionally shortlist properties for later review
    \item Decide whether to pursue the rental
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{User B: Leaving a Review}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Landing / Home page $\rightarrow$ ``Leave a Review'' CTA
    \item Search for and select the property
    \item Complete structured rating form (five metrics, half-star precision)
    \item Optionally add private context (500 character cap)
    \item Provide tenancy period
    \item Submit review (enters \texttt{pending} moderation queue)
    \item Receive submission confirmation
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{User C: Landlord Managing Portfolio (New)}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Sign in with landlord account
    \item Access Business Portal from header navigation
    \item View portfolio dashboard with property cards and trust scores
    \item Drill into a specific property for category performance analytics
    \item View benchmark comparison against city/neighbourhood averages
    \item Review and respond to tenant reviews (draft enters admin approval queue)
    \item Check review gap alerts; generate invite links for under-reviewed properties
    \item Manage team members and notification preferences
\end{enumerate}

\section{Dependencies}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{User adoption}: Initial data collection depends on ability to pitch to building companies. Renter participation depends on emphasising value.
    \item \textbf{Data quality}: Honest, consistent structured inputs to ensure scoring integrity.
    \item \textbf{Legal and policy}: Compliance with Canadian and provincial housing, privacy, and anti-discrimination laws. Building-focused, not individual-focused.
    \item \textbf{Trust and safety}: Email verification, abuse mitigation, and moderation to prevent misuse.
    \item \textbf{Clear value proposition}: Renters need enough data to keep using the platform. Landlords must receive measurable benefits to remain engaged.
    \item \textbf{Slices 01--17}: Core product must be complete before Business Portal and consumer UX expansion (Slice 50).
\end{itemize}
